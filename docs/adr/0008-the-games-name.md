# The game's name: name the miss, and gate the slate on ownability

"Mind-Meld" was a working label that quietly became the project's name, carrying a tracker directory, a spec, three ADRs, two research notes and a deploy subdomain. Research on 2026-09-03 (`docs/research/name-mind-meld-availability.md`) retired it as a shipped name on four independent grounds — a live US Class 28 registration reciting "party games involving word challenges", an app-store collision, zero distinctiveness, and the taste argument that it names the win condition when Stage 0 found **the harsh grade is the punchline**. Decision (2026-09-04): the name **points at the miss, in the concede register** — the players' shrug at a grade they cannot argue with, not the Assayer's measurement of the gap — and no candidate reaches my wife unless it first passes an **ownability gate**. The pick itself is hers, from a four-name slate, and this ADR stays *proposed* until she has played a Page.

## The gate

Adopted once distribution became a real possibility. It filters the *slate*, before taste sees a candidate; it is never a veto applied after she has chosen. A name passes only if all five hold:

1. Zero live trademark records in Nice classes 9, 28 and 41 in any register.
2. No existing **game** carrying the name in any store.
3. An unclaimed `<name> game` search query.
4. At least one good domain form available.
5. **Spellable on hearing** — word of mouth is the whole distribution channel for a couch game.

The slate: **Nearabouts**, **Wellnigh**, **Thereish**, **Fairish**. Presented cold, without the case for any of them, and again after she has played a Page.

## Decision (2026-09-05): Thereish

My wife heard the slate cold on 2026-09-04 and again after playing, and picked **Thereish** both times — so Falsifier 2 did not fire. She added that she would prefer a name pointed at the game's goal over one pointed at the near miss, and Jacob, who prefers the miss, wanted that direction tried before settling. It was: `docs/research/name-candidate-clearance-round4.md` gated Jinx, Same Page, Unison, Chime and Kindred, and every one fails on facts — live marks in Nice 28 or 41, exact-title games on BGG or Steam, and two products launched in August 2026 that ship this exact mechanic under two of those names (*Same Page*, *Unison*). Goal-direction words are ordinary English for agreement, which is what companies name themselves; the miss-direction coinage was available for the same reason. **The game is Thereish**, for now and by Jacob's call in light of the register. **Deep Meld** is the recorded runner-up — clean on every register and store, 9 of 10 domain forms free — held off because it is the ladder's top rung and would face a similarity read against MIND MELD reg. 7540401 on identical goods. The rule that the title never reuses a glossary word was relaxed for the title's sake during this round; Thereish reuses none, so nothing on the ladder renames. If Falsifier 3 ever fires, Deep Meld is the first name to re-clear.

## Considered options

Recorded because most of these will be suggested again otherwise. Cleared across four research notes: `name-mind-meld-availability.md`, `name-candidate-clearance.md`, `name-candidate-clearance-round2.md`, `name-candidate-clearance-round3.md`.

- **Near Enough** — Jacob's and my wife's shared favourite, and the reason the gate exists. It fails criteria 2 and 3: a live monetised Google Play game is titled exactly that, and a common laudatory phrase can be neither coined nor searched nor enforced. **Nearenough** closed up does not escape the same blocker.
- **Fair Enough** — the sharpest fit to the Stage 0 datum on paper, killed by the only instrument that matters: My wife reads it as "that logic makes sense", not as granting something in good humour.
- **Nighabouts** — won every measurable axis in the whole series (zero records worldwide, no game anywhere, 9 of 9 domains free including the exact `.com`) and lost on criterion 5. It is pronounced NYE-abouts; an unclaimed query nobody can spell is a locked room, not an empty one.
- **Thereabouts** — register-clean, but `thereabouts.app` is a pre-launch couples app heading for the same store, and `thereabouts.gg` is a two-player near-miss guessing game.
- **Ballpark**, **Nearish**, **Approx** — each already occupied by a shipped game of this exact shape. The recurring lesson: the "how close did you get" genre keeps reaching for the same six idioms, so searching the category returns the category's names.
- **Middling / Passable / Tolerable** — the words critics use for a mediocre game. Unwinnable queries, and the name would read as the product's own review. **Passable survives as a Band name around 50**, which is where the joke belongs.
- **Deep Meld**, **Two Heads**, **The Word Between Us**, **Assay**, **Betwixt**, **Halfway** — from the original shortlist. Dropped for naming the win, scaling badly past two Seats, failing the say-aloud test, reading as lab jargon, prior products, and a Steam collision respectively.
- **Give or Take** — the purest undersell idiom found, killed by the standing rule that the title may never reuse a glossary word. **Give** is a defined term, and the Chronicle is append-only and player-visible, so the ambiguity would be paid forever.

## Consequences

- **Meld stays** in the glossary and on the ladder even though it leaves the title. Being the generic name for this kind of game is what makes it a good word for players and a bad name for a product.
- The **Band** formerly called *near* is renamed **close**, so the title cannot collide with a rung of its own ladder.
- `meld.stephens.page` stands until the name is final, then changes **exactly once** — it is the only part of the rename carrying a DNS record, a vhost and a TLS cert. No candidate-specific subdomain in the meantime.
- Domain purchase is back in scope now that distribution is plausible; trademark filing remains out of scope and is an attorney's question, not this project's.
- **Bought (2026-09-05): `thereish.app`**, pointed at the droplet; the game moves there from `meld.stephens.page` (naming issue 07). `thereish.com` is parked dark by a speculator until 2026-12-18 and is not approached; if it drops, it is registered then and redirected.

## Falsifiers

Three things would reopen this:

1. **My wife vetoes**, with no reason required. "My wife likes it" is this project's success criterion everywhere else; asking her to justify taste to a spec would break it here.
2. **Her cold pick and her post-Page pick disagree** — that would mean the evening's pleasure is the convergence rather than the miss, and the concede-versus-measure axis was chosen wrong. No reshuffling of this slate fixes that; it sends the decision back to the fork.
3. **The game gets good enough to distribute.** Everything here was cleared against a private-use standard. Re-run clearance before announcing anything.
