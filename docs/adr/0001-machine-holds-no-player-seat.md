# The machine holds no player seat

Our research (docs/research/) validated that an LLM can staff the "free language" seats that leave group word games broken at two players. We are deliberately not building that. Jacob's rule: if a human could do a role better, the role stays human — or the design waits for more humans (a crowd-shaped game should be played with a crowd, not a simulated one). The machine takes only jobs where it beats any possible human: authoring boards nightly for an audience of two, keeping the Chronicle (remembering every session, forever, and weaving it back), and measuring meaning-distance instantly, gradedly, and consistently. It is the couple's live-in game designer, scribe, and instrument — never a stand-in player.

## Considered options

- **LLM as the missing player or judge** (e.g., refereeing Taboo for two, where the rules give the buzzer to opposing players): rejected as human-simulation. A person does that job better and enjoys doing it.
- **LLM as author + scribe + instrument**: accepted. No human can construct personalized boards on demand, remember everything without fatigue or favoritism, or grade semantic closeness the same way twice.

## Consequences

Mechanic candidates are judged first by this test. It also implies human final authority everywhere: the players can jointly overrule any machine output, and the overrule is recorded in the Chronicle.
