# Calls and Reactions: stakes and noise in a game that keeps no score

Thereish has no score and will not have one: the Closeness is a punchline, not a tally, and design law 8's graded ladder is the whole of the co-op's arithmetic. That left the Beat as dead air — a fixed pause in which the table waits for the Assayer — and the reveal as a moment the phones had no part in. Decision (2026-10-23): a Seat may **Call** a band before the number arrives — from its own commit, while the others write, until the Beat ends — and may send one of four **Reactions** at the reveal — ooh, how, no way, yes. Both are legitimate in a scoreless game for one reason: **nothing accumulates.**

Precisely, and these are the rules that keep it true:

- Nothing is totalled. No count, rate, streak or running tally of Calls or Reactions is rendered anywhere — not on a phone, not on the Stage, not in the book.
- Nothing persists beyond the Chronicle. A Call lives on its Attempt and a Reaction on its reveal; neither crosses to the next Page, the next night or a Seat's history.
- No grading role reads them. Neither enters an assay payload, so the Precedent key is untouched (ADR-0006). The Scribe hears the Reactions, because the Scribe writes the evening and the noise around a reveal *is* the evening; the Author and the Assayer never see either.
- A Call settles nothing. Being right is worth nothing but the pin sitting where the marker lands, and a Seat that does not call simply has no pin on the Rail.
- The window is the existing Beat. Calling never gates the reveal: the Beat is as long as it was, the Assayer grades inside it, and a table that calls nothing waits the same.

What they buy is a stake in the thirty seconds that had none — a reason to look up from your own word and read everyone else's — and, at the reveal, a way for six people to make noise at once without six people shouting over the Stage.

## Considered options

- **Points for a correct Call.** Rejected, and this is the decision the rest follows from: a point is a tally, a tally wants a leaderboard, and the first leaderboard turns a co-op meaning game into a quiz.
- **Calling a number rather than a band.** Rejected: a number is a guess at the Assayer and reads as competing with it. A band is a reading of the table.
- **Free-text reactions.** Rejected: free text at a reveal is a second quip competing with the Assayer's, and the Quip is the punchline. Four words are a vocabulary the Stage can draw and a hand can feel.
- **A Reaction tied to a Seat's Opinion of the grade.** Rejected: an Opinion is evidence about the Assayer (ADR-0019) and must stay deliberate. A reaction at a punchline is not evidence of anything.

## Consequences

- The four Reactions are the Game's vocabulary, read from there by every screen and by the haptics, so one list feeds the buttons, the float on the Stage and the buzz.
- Each lands on its Attempt in the Chronicle, in roster order, and the book shows them with no count beside them.
- A Reaction is felt as well as seen: the buzz is a Cue the phone takes whether or not it holds the Speaker, which is the one Cue that is not the Speaker's.
- The fairness tap and the Bookends taps leave the punchline for the closing card, so the reveal's seconds belong to the Call, the Rail and the Quip. ADR-0019 is untouched: feedback still never rewrites recorded play.

Amended 2026-10-24, after the first local run: a window of the Beat alone (2.5 s) was too short to read the Bookends and choose, and it left the first Seat to commit with nothing to do while the others wrote. The window now opens on a Seat's own commit. A Call made before the Beat is shown only to its caller, so a confident band cannot steer a Bridge still being written; at the Beat every Call becomes public.
