# Bait bridges are Thereish's misdirection engine, shipped as an A/B layer

Stage 0 retired the clover candidate (unplayed) and left Thereish (then under the working title Mind-Meld), which carried no misdirection at all — and design law 9 says only two tricks survive at two players (overlapping keys, blind decoys), while Melissa's signature pleasure is beating a fair trap and narrating it afterwards. Decision (2026-09-02): the Author plants Anchors that have an obvious **Bait** bridge and at least two deeper bridges; converging on the bait is still a Meld, on the lowest rung, and converging on a deep bridge is the top rung. A miss is never a loss. It ships as an A/B layer — bait-scored Pages against plain ones — so the couch decides whether misdirection was what Thereish lacked.

## Considered options

- **Decoy anchor** (three words, one impostor; converge on *which two* before bridging): a double convergence, heavier to play and harder to grade fairly.
- **Private lenses** (Duet-style overlapping keys — each phone sees a different lens the meld must satisfy): the strongest second bet if bait proves too subtle.
- **Authored walks** (a Page as a path through a meaning-space, forks carrying the misdirection): the most Chapter-native and the most build.

## Consequences

The Author's `likely_bridges` and `why_these` become load-bearing content — the narration of the trap. The bait rung must remain a meld, or the game punishes the obviousness that convergence games reward.

## Superseded (2026-09-05)

Retired by ADR-0011 after the second playtest: the one Bait that fired that night (lighthouse/lighthouse, graded 75 on the bait rung) was tapped "too harsh" by both players, and with Bookends chosen to stand far apart and Gives kept private, the misdirection the Bait supplied comes from the Page itself. The A/B harness stays; its first experiment is now the Author's model.
