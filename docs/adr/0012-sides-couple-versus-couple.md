# Sides: Couple versus couple as two paired Seats on shared Bookends

ADR-0010 chose one Verdict per Attempt for group play, reading all N Bridges together to keep the reveal concise. When two couples play in one Room, however, players often want a rival-team race (design law 6: the crowd's jobs include rival-team races; Melissa enjoys competitive play). This ADR introduces **Sides**: Seats can form Sides (two Sides of two Seats in a four-seat Room) to play the same Bookends as two competing units.

## Considered options

- **Strict competitive scoring**: Primary focus on winner/loser delta and zero-sum rankings. Rejected: Thereish is fundamentally a co-op meaning game, and competitive hostility detracts from the comedy of misinterpretation.
- **Independent converging arcs**: Each couple carries forward their own pair of Bridges, causing their Bookends to diverge after Attempt 1. Rejected: Playing on diverging Bookends splits the Room into two separate games rather than a shared Room experience.
- **Side-by-side co-op ladders with shared converging arc** (chosen): Both Sides play on the same Bookends and receive separate within-side Verdicts. The phone reveal displays both Sides' Closeness, band, and quip side-by-side, noting who was closer or if either melded. If neither melds, carry-forward remains one shared converging arc: the two Bridges furthest apart overall become the next Bookends for everyone.

## Consequences

Seats choose partners to form Sides in the Room before play begins ("I'm with Melissa"). When a 4-seat Room is partitioned into two Sides of two, the Assayer grades within-side pairs (two Verdicts). The reveal presents two Closeness numbers side-by-side. A Page melds if either Side reaches the meld threshold. An overrule requires agreement of the Side's members. At two Seats, or when Sides are not declared, the standard single-verdict rules (ADR-0010) remain completely unchanged.
