# Prior art: LLMs (and adjacent language tech) as judge, referee, guesser, GM, or constructor in word/meaning games

Researched 2026-08-31 for the meaning-game design brief (LLM third seat: referee / constructor / decoy-master; legacy memory). Method: every claim traces to a primary source — the game/store page, the creator's own interview/postmortem/talk, official docs, or the paper. Secondary or anecdotal evidence is labeled as such. Reception labels: **hit** (mass audience), **niche** (real but small audience), **flopped/mixed** (shipped, poorly received), **unproven** (too new/thin evidence).

---

## Shipped games

### Infinite Craft (Neal Agarwal, Jan 2024) — LLM as meaning-combiner and de facto judge

**Role.** Combine any two elements; [Llama 2 70B (with Llama Guard) on Together AI](https://www.llama.com/community-stories) invents the result — peaking at ~650k tokens/sec. Every pair is computed once and cached, so [the same pair always yields the same result for all players, and the first player to hit a new combination gets a "First Discovery" label](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinite_Craft). The LLM is simultaneously content generator and semantic judge (its answer *is* the ruling on what fire + water means).
**Reception.** Hit — arguably the first breakout game with GenAI as core mechanic; [#3 most-searched game on Google in 2024](https://speedrun.substack.com/p/three-ai-apps-that-deserve-the-hype); Agarwal: ["I'm never going to financially recover from this"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinite_Craft) (tweet, Feb 2024).
**Why.** The judge's wrongness is the content: PC Gamer's launch coverage celebrated escalation to ["God, the Big Bang, and 'Yin-Yoda'"](https://www.pcgamer.com/this-browser-based-endless-crafting-game-starts-you-off-with-fire-and-water-but-it-quickly-escalates-to-god-the-big-bang-and-yin-yoda/); Agarwal himself noted Llama is ["not quite as smart" as ChatGPT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinite_Craft) — and shipped anyway, because surprise beats correctness in a sandbox. Caching converts a stochastic judge into a deterministic-feeling world (no player ever sees the same input judged two ways) and makes the cost curve amortize. Agarwal, in [a16z's speedrun interview](https://speedrun.substack.com/p/three-ai-apps-that-deserve-the-hype): "The hardest part with Infinite Craft was managing costs and scaling… there aren't many best practices yet for a game built on LLMs." First Discovery is retention gold that falls straight out of the cache-miss check.

### Death by AI (Playroom, Nov 2023) — LLM as party-game referee

**Role.** Jackbox-style: everyone types a survival plan for an absurd scenario; the AI narrates each plan's outcome and rules live/die. Free, web/mobile/[Discord activity](https://discord.com/build-case-studies/playroom), 1–10 players.
**Reception.** Hit — [~7M users within weeks of the Discord launch, 1.2B AI tokens generated](https://discord.com/build-case-studies/playroom) (Discord/Playroom case study); [20M players and per-user costs low enough for profitability, per its AI vendor Inworld](https://inworld.ai/case-study/how-inworld-helped-the-ai-game-death-by-ai-with-20-million-players-reach-profitability) (vendor case study — treat numbers accordingly). App Store rating 4.2/5 (small n=45).
**Why.** The framing does the fairness work: the store copy sells an unaccountable judge as the premise — ["it's not programmed to care!"](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/death-by-ai-party-game/id6474457831). Wrong or exploitable rulings become group comedy: in the [launch HN thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=38318889) players openly prompt-inject ("I survive") and the developer treats it as part of the fun rather than a bug to close; the only acknowledged bug class is the judge *failing to decide*. Note the ceiling (anecdotal, App Store review): a player named "the devil" kept being ruled dead against the narrated outcome — funny once, corrosive if the game asked for real cleverness. Latency is absorbed structurally: all verdicts for a round are generated in one batch and revealed as theater. Judged verdicts + shared screen + short rounds = the closest shipped analog to "LLM referee at a party," and it worked by *lowering* the stakes of judgment, not by making judgment good.

### Suck Up! (Proxima, Dec 2023 web → 1.0 Steam Oct 2025) — LLM NPCs as persuadable jury

**Role.** You're a vampire; you improvise (by voice) at the doors of [ChatGPT-powered residents](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2726370/Suck_Up/) who decide whether to invite you in — each NPC is effectively a judge of your rhetoric.
**Reception.** Hit at launch, mixed at 1.0. Viral streamer wave from [the Dec 12, 2023 release](https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/subcultures/suck-up) (Know Your Meme timeline — community-documented; multi-million-view Let's Plays within weeks). Proxima's Nick Zak, [Oct 2025 interview](https://www.hypergridbusiness.com/2025/10/indie-vampire-game-highlights-future-for-ai-driven-games/): "I've personally watched or joined over a thousand streams, and I'm always struck by how distinct each one feels." But the Steam 1.0 sits at [Mixed — 62% of ~203 reviews](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2726370/Suck_Up/), with reviewers (anecdotal) blaming a model/token-economy change for NPCs that got dumber than in early access.
**Why worked, then sagged.** The judge's improvisation is the comedy engine — great for performing to an audience, thinner as a private loop. Hilary Mason's field survey ([The State of AI-Native Games, Apr 2026](https://medium.com/@hmason/the-state-of-ai-native-games-lessons-from-the-frontier-3e696a9e3279)) uses it for the key lesson: **conversational freedom ≠ narrative freedom** — a top Steam review complains "There are only two actual decisions that affect the final outcome." Players eventually feel whether the judge's verdict actually routes anywhere. Also a live warning about judge quality being an *ops* variable: cheaper inference silently degraded the game years after launch (anecdotal reviews, but consistent).

### Gandalf (Lakera, 2023) — LLM as adversarial gatekeeper (adjacent: security game, but a pure language duel)

**Role.** Seven-plus levels of an LLM guarding a password; you talk it into leaking. The "referee" is the defense stack itself.
**Reception.** Hit for what it is — Lakera says [over a million players, "the world's largest red team"](https://www.lakera.ai/ai-security-guides/ai-red-teaming-insights-from-the-worlds-largest-red-team); the game yielded a [279k-prompt attack dataset and a research paper on the security–utility tradeoff](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.07927) (*Gandalf the Red*, 2025).
**Why.** Inverts the fairness problem: the machine's inconsistency *is* the puzzle — you probe a fickle judge until it cracks, so arbitrariness reads as depth, not injustice. The level ladder gives a crisp progression metric on top of a mushy language interaction. Also proof that a language duel with an LLM can be self-motivating with zero narrative dressing.

### Semantle (David Turner, Jan 2022) & Contexto (Nildo Junior, 2022) — the embeddings-judge lineage (**not** LLMs)

**Distinction.** These judge with *static* word embeddings (word2vec / GloVe): precomputed, deterministic, zero marginal cost, zero latency — and incapable of explaining themselves. Parked as off-thesis in the brief, but they're the best-documented evidence on machine semantic judgment and daily ritual.
**Role.** Guess the secret word; the machine scores each guess's semantic proximity (Semantle: cosine similarity 0–100; Contexto: rank 1–N).
**Reception.** Both viral daily hits, then decay to stable niches. Turner: peak was ["a quarter of a million unique IPs every day" before declining](https://www.hey.gg/blog/semantle). Contexto: [300k players/day at Brazil peak; 90% of surveyed players discovered it via friends/social; "I play your game with my grandma. Every day it is something that we do together now"](https://www.hey.gg/blog/contexto).
**Why — fairness mechanics.** Turner is candid that the judge is skewed by training data: word2vec was trained on news, so ["belt" scores as boxing](https://www.hey.gg/blog/semantle) — his mitigations were transparency plus a **hint button** (a soft appeal: "a word slightly better than your best guess," per the [official FAQ](https://semantle.com/faq)), and the FAQ openly disclaims quirks (stop-words removed because "they were just warping the scores"; UK spellings "you're on your own"). Junior's bigger fix: replace the raw similarity score with **rank**, because "the only important thing for the player is knowing how much closer or farther away they are" ([hey.gg interview](https://www.hey.gg/blog/contexto)) — a monotonic, comparative signal feels fair even when the underlying space is weird. Determinism matters too: the judge may be odd, but it's *consistently* odd, so players can learn it.

### Semantris (Google, 2018) & Say What You See (Google Arts & Culture, 2023) — machine as guesser / soft scorer (adjacent, pre-LLM and multimodal)

**Role.** [Semantris](https://research.google/blog/introducing-semantic-experiences-with-talk-to-books-and-semantris/): a dual-encoder semantic model *ranks* on-screen words against your free association — the machine is the guesser; you learn to be legible to it. [Say What You See](https://blog.google/company-news/outreach-and-initiatives/arts-culture/google-arts-culture-new-ai-games/) (by artist-in-residence Jack Wild): describe an AI image in ≤120 chars; you pass a level at a 50%→60%→70% similarity threshold, three attempts, with tips.
**Reception.** Both demo-scale experiments (no retention story claimed); notable as design patterns, not businesses.
**Why.** Two durable fairness devices: (1) *the human adapts to the machine* — when the machine is the guesser, its errors read as your clue being unclear, not its ruling being unjust; (2) *percentage thresholds with retries* turn a fuzzy judgment into a negotiable score. Both fit a couch game better than binary verdicts.

### Blabrecs (Max Kreminski & Isaac Karth, 2020) — classifier as absurdist rules-lawyer (**not** an LLM)

**Role.** Scrabble inverted: only *nonsense* words are legal, and legality is decided by in-browser classifiers (a Markov model and a CNN) trained on the English dictionary — you must sound English without being English ([AIIDE '21 demo paper](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/view/18917), [NeurIPS 2023 creative track](https://mkremins.github.io/publications/Blabrecs_NeurIPS2023.pdf), [play page](https://mkremins.github.io/blabrecs/)).
**Reception.** Niche/cult; the authors report wide social sharing since Dec 2020 (self-reported in the paper).
**Why.** The most elegant answer yet to "what if the judge is arbitrary?": make the arbitrariness *the rule system*, and give players a **pre-commitment probe** — the "test a word…" box lets you ask the judge before you stake anything, so a capricious ruling costs curiosity, not points. Players argue *with* the classifier for laughs and develop folk theories of its taste. Judgment-as-terrain rather than judgment-as-verdict.

### AI Dungeon (Nick Walton / Latitude, 2019–present) — LLM as narrator-GM; the appeal-loop and memory precedent

**Role.** Free-text adventure narrated by an LLM (GPT-2 → [GPT-3 in July 2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI_Dungeon) → multi-model marketplace today).
**Reception.** Hit, then a long messy middle, still alive in 2026. [100k+ players within a week of the Dec 2019 relaunch; 1.5M by mid-2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI_Dungeon). Walton on why it suddenly worked: ["The very first version… was coherent on a sentence level, but on a paragraph level it made no sense… Once you get to GPT-3, it's a lot more coherent on a story level"](https://techcrunch.com/2021/02/04/latitude-seed-funding/). The [April 2021 content-filter crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI_Dungeon) (false positives like "eight-year-old laptop," human review of private stories, review-bombing — per Wired/Vice/Polygon reporting cited there) remains the canonical cautionary tale for moderating a private language game. Retired from Steam 2024; web/app continue ([Phoenix update, model roster in official help center](https://help.aidungeon.com/faq/the-basics)).
**Why — two structural inventions.** (1) The **self-serve appeal loop**: official controls are Take a Turn / Continue / **Retry** / **Undo** / **Edit** / **Erase** ([guidebook](https://help.aidungeon.com/faq/the-basics)) — every bad ruling by the narrator is instantly correctable by the player, which converts "the AI is wrong" from a grievance into an editing gesture. (2) **Player-visible memory**: Remember / World Info let players pin what the narrator must not forget — an admission, all the way back in 2020, that silent LLM memory fails and the fix is a *shared, editable artifact*.

### Hidden Door (Hilary Mason & co., public launch Aug 2025) — constrained narrator with a state database

**Role.** Social tabletop-style roleplay in licensed fiction worlds; an AI narrator runs the story, but generation is decomposed and fenced: ["The machine is not itself creative. The creativity comes from our authors"](https://aigamechangers.substack.com/p/the-machine-is-not-itself-creative) — tens of thousands of curated tropes, a Postgres world-state DB that *programmatically* rejects impossible actions (the Ferris-wheel-in-pocket example), quantized/self-hosted models, and Mason's cost mantra: "It's about turning generation problems into ranking problems."
**Reception.** [Public launch Aug 2025](https://variety.com/2025/gaming/news/hidden-door-ai-role-playing-fan-fiction-game-platform-1236488265/) after long beta; too early for a verdict — call it credible-niche. Mason's [GDC 2026 talk](https://schedule.gdconf.com/session/the-state-of-ai-native-games/915367) and [essay](https://medium.com/@hmason/the-state-of-ai-native-games-lessons-from-the-frontier-3e696a9e3279) are the field's best designer-written state-of-play.
**Why it matters here.** The narrator earns trust by *citing state*: rulings come from a database the fiction actually keeps, not vibes. Persistence is a feature: burn down the Emerald City and it stays burned for future stories; returning players get a "Last time on…" recap ([interview](https://aigamechangers.substack.com/p/the-machine-is-not-itself-creative)). Licensing forced discipline: "No one will hand you their beloved characters unless you can guarantee they won't say and do horrible things" ([essay](https://medium.com/@hmason/the-state-of-ai-native-games-lessons-from-the-frontier-3e696a9e3279)).

### Friends & Fables (Fables, ~2023–present) — commercial LLM game-master with legible memory

**Role.** "Franz," an AI GM for D&D-ish campaigns: [narrates, tracks HP/inventory, runs skill checks and spells, "remembers long-term details"](https://fables.gg/blog/what-makes-the-friends--fables-ai-game-generator-so-good); multiplayer campaigns; subscription business.
**Reception.** Niche but durable — active community and third-party hands-on reviews into 2026 (e.g., [this 2026 review](https://dungeonsdeep.ai/blog/friends-and-fables-review-2026); secondary source, competitor-adjacent site — treat as anecdotal). That review documents the memory design: automatic memories every ~5 turns retrieved into a **player-visible, player-editable "Working Context"** with manual pinning and @mentions.
**Why.** The strongest shipped example of *legacy memory as product surface*: memory is not a hidden context trick but an inspectable document the players co-own. Mechanical bookkeeping (dice, HP) is handled by code, not the LLM — the LLM narrates and adjudicates fiction while deterministic systems hold the ledger.

### Dead Meat (Meaning Machine, demo 2024 → full release planned 2026) & Hex Judge (Jul 2026) — the interrogation / "first-person talker" genre

**Role.** Dead Meat: type or say *anything* to an AI murder suspect; instruments — a lie-detector reading stance and an audible inner monologue — turn free conversation into evidence you can act on; win by pinning who/where/how/why ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2628740/Dead_Meat/)). The AI is heavily authored: "AI-enabled dialogue is only as good as the humans behind it" — hand-written case bibles ground every response ([PreMortem interview](https://premortem.games/2024/01/19/literally-talk-to-the-murder-suspect-in-dead-meat-ai-enabled-dialogue-is-only-as-good-as-the-humans-behind-it/)); they presented [on-device small-language-model NPCs at GDC 2025 with NVIDIA ACE](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/on-demand/session/gdc25-gdc1010/) (latency/cost/offline). Pre-release press: EDGE "Unlike anything else we've ever played"; TheGamer credits a [new genre, the "first-person talker"](https://www.thegamer.com/dead-meat-meaning-machine-interview-proper-conversations-fictional-murderers/). Reception: unproven until release.
**Hex Judge**: $5.99 witch-trial interrogation game that [ships a local LLM inside the game — fully offline, 8 languages, 30+ villagers whose testimonies shift under your questioning](https://www.moddb.com/games/hex-judge/news/hex-judge-launches-on-steam-july-16-medieval-witch-trial-investigation-powered-by-a-local-llm) ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/4508120/Hex_Judge/), July 16, 2026). Reception: unproven (just shipped). Notable purely as proof that a bundled local model at an impulse price point is now shippable.
**Why.** This genre answers "free text but fair" by *instrumenting* the conversation (lie detector, inner voice = legible state) and by authored grounding — the designers, not the model, decide what's true.

### 1001 Nights (Ada Eden / Yuqian Sun, demo Oct 2024) — LLM narrator whose words become game pieces

**Role.** You are Shahrzad; you steer the LLM-driven King's story so that he *says* weapon words ("sword," "shield") — spoken words materialize as items you fight him with ([AIIDE 2023 paper, GPT-4 with consistency scaffolding](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.12915); [official site](https://www.1001nights.ai/)).
**Reception.** Critical/festival darling, commercial niche: [Steam demo "Very Positive" (90% of 130 reviews)](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2782660/1001_Nights_Demo/); exhibited at [FACT Liverpool](https://www.fact.co.uk/artwork/1001-nights-2024) and festivals (A MAZE recognition per event listings — secondary).
**Why.** The purest "language as material" mechanic on record: the player wins by making the machine *mean* something specific — coaxing rather than commanding. The win condition (did the target word appear?) is string-matchable, so a fuzzy co-writing loop terminates in an objectively checkable event. That hybrid — soft process, hard trigger — is a fairness pattern worth stealing.

### Small indie LLM word games (reception thin — listed for coverage, not evidence)

- **[Taboo AI](https://taboo-ai.com/rule)** (open-source web, 2023–): solo Taboo inverted — you clue, the AI guesses, and gpt-3.5 evaluates your cluing; niche, no meaningful reception coverage found.
- **[Yapword](https://yapword.com/ai-word-game/)** (2026): daily Wordle-like where a Gemini-powered "emperor" gives hints and *roasts your finished board* — LLM as color commentator rather than judge; brand new, unproven, but directly instantiates the brief's "color commentator" seat.
- Post-2023 the pattern-matching space is crowded with LLM-judged microgames (many surfaced only via listicles, e.g. [wordgameai.com](https://wordgameai.com/) — leads only, not evidence); none besides the above showed a traceable reception story.

## Research, competitions, and papers

- **Codenames, three generations.** (1) The [Codenames AI Competition framework](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/view/5239) (Kim, Ruzmaykin, Truong & Summerville, AIIDE-19; [still running at COG 2025](https://cog2025.inesc-id.pt/codenames-ai-competition/)) formalized giver/guesser roles. (2) [Koyyalagunta et al., JAIR 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.05885): embedding clue-givers scored against *humans* guessing; naive embeddings pick alienating clues, and their fix — DETECT, weighting by dictionary senses and document frequency — improved human precision up to 102.8%. Lesson: raw vector-nearness ≠ human-guessable. (3) [Stephenson, Sidji & Ronval, 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.11373): GPT-4o/Claude 3.5/Gemini 1.5/Llama 3.1 as codemasters and guessers are "more generalisable to a wider range of teammates" than embedding agents, show distinct per-model styles, but "can still suffer in lateral thinking challenges."
- **Taboo with LLMs.** ["Don't Say It!"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00601) (Candussio et al., Jul 2026): open models playing Taboo trade rule-compliance against communicativeness depending on how constraints are enforced (prompting vs. decoding vs. representation edits), and LLMs "substantially underperform humans as guessers." Method note for us: they referee via a separate violation-detector plus an LLM judge of clue quality — referee decomposed into a hard check and a soft check.
- **Word-guessing games as LLM evals** became a standard tool in 2023: [GameEval](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.10032) (Ask-Guess, SpyFall variants), [SpyGame](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.20499) (word-disguise "Who is the Spy").
- **Kaggle "LLM 20 Questions"** ([2024, $50k](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/llm-20-questions)): paired cooperating agents; the winning meta ([1st-place writeup](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/llm-20-questions/writeups/c-number-1st-place-solution); [ML Contests' season review](https://mlcontests.com/state-of-machine-learning-competitions-2024/), secondary) was **binary search over a fixed vocabulary**, not semantic dialogue — when the stakes were real, competitors routed *around* LLM semantic judgment because it was the unreliable part.
- **CALYPSO** ([Zhu, Martin, Head & Callison-Burch, AIIDE 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.07540)): real D&D DMs given GPT-3 tools wanted an *assistant*, not an autopilot — the system worked when it produced presentable prose and half-formed ideas the DM could own; the paper stresses preserving DM/player agency. The strongest academic backing for a "third seat that serves the human table" rather than replaces it.
- **Surveys.** [Gallotta et al., "LLMs and Games: A Survey and Roadmap" (2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.18659) taxonomizes LLM roles (player, master/referee, NPC, commentator, designer). The [AI-Native Games survey (Jul 2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00527) is blunter about the judge seat: AI judges strain trust "because their decisions may be stochastic, opaque, or inconsistent" — players need explanations *tied to game state*; unpredictable latency "makes the loop feel less like play"; if the model "forgets prior decrees… player actions no longer accumulate into a reliable game state"; and of its 53-game corpus, most are still Early Access/demo — the field has hits but few sustained products.
- **LLM-as-judge fundamentals.** [MT-Bench / Chatbot Arena paper (NeurIPS 2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05685): a strong LLM judge agrees with humans >80% — about the human-human agreement rate — with named biases: position, verbosity, self-enhancement, weak grading of reasoning. I.e., a well-prompted referee is roughly as agreeable as a second human, and its failure modes are known and designable-around.

## Signals for our design

Everything below is **inference** from the above, aimed at the brief's three interests. Facts are linked above; the connecting judgment is ours.

### For an LLM referee two players can appeal

1. **Consistency is the felt substance of fairness, more than accuracy.** The two loved judges — Infinite Craft's (cached, deterministic per pair) and Contexto's (frozen embedding) — are *wrong constantly* but never inconsistent, and players forgive them. The distrusted moments are inconsistency: Death by AI ruling against its own narration, Suck Up's NPCs degrading after a silent model swap. Inference: cache rulings (same clue pair → same ruling forever), pin model+prompt versions per campaign, and treat any model change as a visible "new season."
2. **Binary verdicts are the hardest to make feel fair; prefer graded, comparative, or monotonic output.** Contexto's rank, Say What You See's percentage-with-threshold, Semantle's hint-as-soft-appeal all defuse resentment; Death by AI gets away with binary only because stakes are jokes. For Melissa's clever-aha stakes, score semantic distance / award graded credit, and reserve hard pass–fail for string-checkable events (the 1001 Nights pattern: soft process, hard trigger).
3. **Ship the appeal loop as an interaction, not a policy.** Shipped precedents: AI Dungeon's Retry/Edit/Undo (any ruling is instantly revisable), Blabrecs' "test a word" probe (ask the judge *before* committing), Semantle's hint button. For two players: require the referee to show its reasoning tied to visible game state (the 2026 survey's demand; Hidden Door's DB-cited rulings), then let the two humans jointly overrule it — CALYPSO says the AI seat works best as assistant with human final authority, and with exactly two players, "we both think it's wrong" is a legitimate supreme court. Log overrules; they're signal (and future memory).
4. **Design around known judge biases.** MT-Bench: position, verbosity, self-preference. Concretely: judge the two players' answers in randomized order, judge each blind before comparing, cap answer length, and use rubric-anchored prompts. Agreement ~80% with humans is the realistic ceiling — build the 20% into the fiction (a referee *character* with taste can be argued with; an oracle can only be wrong).
5. **Split the referee into hard and soft components.** The Taboo paper's method (separate violation detector + quality judge) and F&F's code-handles-dice split: let deterministic code check violations (banned word said, time out, word repeated) and let the LLM only do what needs judgment (closeness, cleverness, decoy plausibility). Fewer stochastic rulings = fewer fairness surfaces.
6. **Humor is a stakes-dependent valve, not a universal one.** Death by AI and Blabrecs convert bad rulings to comedy because nothing real is at stake. The brief says wit belongs in content, not slapstick — so for us wrong rulings can't be the joke; they must be rare (narrow rulings), reversible (appeals), or reframed (referee-as-character with owned taste).

### For LLM decoy/board construction

7. **LLMs are now the right tool for human-facing boards; embeddings weren't.** JAIR 2021: vector-near clues alienate humans, fixed only by re-weighting toward common senses; the 2024 benchmark: LLM givers/guessers generalize to varied human-like partners. Inference: have the LLM construct boards/decoys *and* justify each decoy's misdirection in hidden notes — the justification doubles as the post-round reveal (Melissa's documented pleasure: narrating the trap afterward).
8. **Constructor ≠ live generator: pre-generate, rank, curate.** Mason's "turning generation problems into ranking problems," Hidden Door's offline trope curation, Dead Meat's authored case bibles, CALYPSO's "low-fidelity ideas the DM develops." Generate decks/boards ahead of the evening (cost/latency → zero at the table), have the LLM rank its own candidates, and let Jacob (or last session's memory) cull. Live generation is only for what must respond to the couple's play.
9. **First-discovery credit is cheap and potent.** Infinite Craft instrumented the cache miss into a badge. Couple-scale analog: the game recognizes "no one (of us) has ever bridged these two words before" — novelty detection against your own play history, which the legacy files make trivial.

### For persistent session memory (legacy)

10. **Memory must be a visible, co-editable artifact, or it will betray you.** The 2026 survey names silent forgetting as the trust-killer; every surviving product converged on legible memory: AI Dungeon's Remember/World Info (2020), F&F's player-editable Working Context, Hidden Door's DB. Inference: make the couple's memory file a first-class, readable, editable object *in the fiction* (a shared lexicon, an atlas of bridges built) — not an opaque context blob.
11. **Persistence should change future play, and recap should open the session.** Hidden Door: burned cities stay burned; "Last time on…" recaps re-onboard returning players. For an evenings-cadence couch game, a 20-second "previously, you two…" generated from the memory file is both ritual and retention.
12. **The memory artifact can replace the share card.** The daily-viral games ran on same-puzzle-for-everyone share artifacts (Contexto's grandma ritual; Semantle's peak-and-decay shows the mechanic alone doesn't hold). Couch play removed sharing from our brief — but the legacy file is the artifact turned inward: the audience is the couple's future selves. Same psychological loop (visible accumulating history, streaks, firsts), no public required.

### Latency / cost

13. **At two players, cost is a rounding error; latency is the real design constraint.** Patterns that hide it: batch all judgments and reveal as one dramatized verdict (Death by AI); precompute boards (see 8); cache repeat rulings (Infinite Craft); stream the commentator but not the referee. Local small models are now a shipped reality (Hex Judge's bundled LLM; Dead Meat's NVIDIA ACE SLM work) — viable fallback if the couch WiFi or API bill ever matters, at some quality cost (see 1: don't swap models silently).

### The gap this game would occupy
