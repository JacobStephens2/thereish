# Two-player communication mechanics — atoms, failures, and the third seat

Research for the meaning game ([design brief](../design-brief.md)), 2026-08-31. Question: which mechanic atoms of word/communication games survive at exactly two players, and which beloved games break there and why — the gap the LLM-third-seat thesis serves.

**Method.** Official rulebook PDFs and publisher pages first; designer interviews second; BoardGameGeek only for factual metadata; community opinion labeled as such. Compiled from four source passes over the official documents linked inline; Balderdash and base-Codenames counts spot-verified against [Mattel's PDF](https://service.mattel.com/instruction_sheets/b9176-0910.pdf) and [czechgames.com](https://czechgames.com/en/codenames/) directly.

---

## Part A — atoms that survive (or were built for) two

### Codenames Duet — symmetric clue-binding under shared time
- **Atom:** one word + number binds multiple hidden targets; both players are simultaneously spymaster and guesser against overlapping keys.
- **Count:** officially **2+** ([CGE page](https://czechgames.com/en/codenames-duet/), [press factsheet](https://www.czechgames.com/for-press-games/codenames-duet-2017)). Rulebook: "This game was designed for 2 players" ([official rules PDF, archived](https://web.archive.org/web/2022id_/https://czechgames.com/files/rules/codenames-duet-rules-en.pdf), p.11).
- **How 2 works:** double-sided key card in a stand — "each player sees only one side"; 9 green + 3 assassins + 13 bystanders per side; "There are 9 on each side, but 3 overlap" → 15 targets; of your 3 assassins, on the other side "one is black, one is green, and one is tan." The rival team is replaced by a shared economy of 9 timer tokens and a mission map (Prague 9 turns/9 mistakes → Berlin 11/2).
- **Why it sings at 2:** the pressure source (being out-clued by rivals) is swapped for a clock both players share, and the overlapping keys keep hidden information and *fair misdirection* alive — your safe word may be my assassin. No seat is deleted; both players give clues, so neither role exhausts.
- **Origin gold:** co-designer Scot Eaton built it because base Codenames locked couples out: "My wife and I love playing games together, but with young kids, we never get to go to game night at the same time" ([The Indie Game Report interview](https://www.theindiegamereport.com/2017/08/talking-codenames-duet-with-scot-eaton/)). Also: "Codenames Duet is tough. Really tough. Expect to lose at least 3 times before you get your first win."

### Wavelength (co-op mode) — spectrum calibration
- **Atom:** encoder maps a hidden point on a continuous axis (hot–cold) to a concept; decoder inverts the mapping. Needs exactly 1 giver + ≥1 guesser.
- **Count:** box **2–12** ([box copy](https://www.amazon.ca/Palm-Court-WAV01-Wavelength/dp/B07T446163); [Wikipedia infobox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wavelength_(game))). The co-op mode in the [official rulebook](https://www.northstreetgames.com/images/Rulebooks/Wavelength-Rulebook.pdf) (retailer-hosted copy) is the official 2P path: "everyone wins or loses together as a single team. It works really well with 2–5 players" — 7-card deck, center hits add bonus cards, "Skip the left/right guessing phase," graded ladder ("16–18 You won! … 22–24 Galaxy brain").
- **Why it works at 2:** the only structurally adversarial piece (the LEFT/RIGHT counter-guess, which needs a second team) is amputated cleanly; the calibration atom is dyad-native. Competitive 2P is impossible — a one-person team has a Psychic and no guessers.
- The official app is co-op only and explicit: "Wavelength supports 2 to 10 players. The game is fully cooperative, so it works great with any group size — even just two people" ([wavelength.zone/app/faq](https://www.wavelength.zone/app/faq)).

### Medium — simultaneous convergence
- **Atom:** two minds iterate toward the same word: count down and speak simultaneously; failed attempts become the next round's public inputs.
- **Count:** officially **2–8** ([Greater Than Games page](https://shop.greaterthangames.com/products/medium)). Official 2P rule (rulebook, "Alternate Rules"): "For two players, use three sets, shuffle in the Crystal Ball cards, don't use ESP cards, play as one team, and count your shared points. Just play for fun!"
- **Why it works at 2:** the atom is dyad-native — normal play is a ring of overlapping two-player dyads anyway. What vanishes at 2 is only the meta-layer (score comparison between pairs); the publisher concedes it: no winner, "just play for fun." Convergence, not competition, is the game.

### Letter Jam — clue-as-construction over visible-to-others letters
- **Atom:** inverted hidden info (see everyone's letter but your own); a spelled word simultaneously encodes positional facts about many hidden letters.
- **Count:** "a cooperative game for **2 to 6** players" ([official rules PDF, archived](https://web.archive.org/web/2022id_/https://czechgames.com/files/rules/letter-jam-rules-en.pdf), p.1; [CGE page](https://czechgames.com/en/letter-jam/)).
- **How 2 works:** artificial seats. "You always use all six stands. If you have fewer than 6 players, some stands will be nonplayer stands" holding card stacks; "Cards in nonplayer stands can be used in any clue, just as if they were players' letters," and emptying a stack earns a bonus clue token. At 2, red-token quotas force both humans to give the first three clues each.
- **Why it works at 2:** the mechanic needs a *table of letters*, not a table of minds — cardboard stands restore the structure; the humans keep all the judgment.

### Hanabi — communication under a rationed, rigid clue grammar
- **Atom:** see every hand but your own; help arrives only via scarce clue tokens with fixed format ("one color OR one value, point to all of them").
- **Count:** "**2 to 5** players" ([R&R Games official rules PDF](https://rnrgames.com/Content/RRGames/images/ProductRules/hanabiRules.PDF)); the only 2P adjustment is hand size (5 cards at 2–3 players).
- **Why 2 is legal but thinner:** the atom scales with how many hands you can see; at 2 each clue has exactly one possible recipient and third-party triangulation ("Alice cluing Bob also informs Carol") disappears. Nothing breaks — the clue economy is count-agnostic — so no dummy seat is needed. Bauza on the design: "its mechanism is based on the acceptance – or not – by the players of a number of intrinsic rules of communication" ([designer blog](https://www.antoinebauza.fr/?p=1646)). Community polls prefer 3–4 (community signal, BGG).

### The Crew — the official artificial third seat (JARVIS / Tonoja)
- **Atom:** cooperative trick-taking under a communication embargo — hidden hands must route specific cards to specific winners with one constrained signal each.
- **Count:** KOSMOS spec **3–5**, "with a special game variant for 2 players" ([Planet Nine](https://www.kosmos.de/en/products/die-crew), [Mission Deep Sea](https://www.kosmos.de/en/products/die-crew-mission-tiefsee)); rulebook: "best when played with a crew of three or more."
- **The 2P variant** ([official T&K rulebook, mirror](https://cdn.1j1ju.com/medias/8f/13/09-the-crew-the-quest-for-planet-nine-rulebook.pdf), pp.19–20): "JARVIS is an artificial intelligence system… treated as a third crew member" — a double row of 14 cards, 7 hidden under 7 face-up; humans hold 13 each. The commander "always decides which of the open cards should be played… The second crew member cannot thereby discuss it"; JARVIS never signals; the card beneath is revealed only after the trick. Deep Sea's version is identical but named Tonoja.
- **Why this matters here:** trick-taking needs three hands of card topology; JARVIS restores the structure at *zero hidden-information cost* — its info is semi-public, it has no mind, and a no-discussion rule keeps the human-to-human channel scarce. The strongest official precedent for "a dummy third seat makes a 3+ structural game work at 2." Industry-wide echo: "An artificial opponent, the Automa, takes the place of a human player in a multiplayer game" ([Automa Factory](https://automafactory.com/about/)).

### So Clover! — clue-binding across word pairs (the atom survives; the table doesn't)
- **Atom:** every clue is doubly constrained — one word must fit two adjacent keywords at once; a blind-drawn decoy card turns reconstruction into joint constraint-satisfaction.
- **Count:** officially **3–6** ([Repos page](https://www.rprod.com/en/games/so-clover); [official rulebook, mirror](https://cdn.1j1ju.com/medias/03/97/c6-so-clover-rulebook.pdf)). Decoy rule: "without looking at it, draw 1 new card and shuffle it with your other 4." Guessing is whole-table-minus-one: "the other players work together"; "If there is a disagreement… the player sitting to the left of the Spectator makes the final decision."
- **At 2:** no official handling, and no designer commentary on counts was found (searched; genuine gap). The write-clues/reconstruct loop is coherent with one writer + one guesser — closest of the group-only games to 2P-viable — but the rules' plural-guesser deliberation (discussion, tie-break) collapses into alternating solitaire. The decoy mechanic is notable: misdirection *without a judge*, self-refereeing via card identity.

### Tick Tock: A Tale for Two — two devices, half the picture each, voice is the network
- **Atom:** split asymmetric information with zero technical link — "you must combine the information on both players' screens… Shout out instructions, discuss what you see, and listen" ([Steam page, developer-written](https://store.steampowered.com/app/790740/Tick_Tock_A_Tale_for_Two/)); each player runs their own copy on their own device ([official site](https://ticktockthegame.com/)).
- **Count:** exactly 2. Origin: "Tick Tock was an investigation of how two people could share a story together" ([PocketGamer.biz interview](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/it-was-surreal-to-see-our-little-brainchild-win-a-google-play-award-and-be-nominated-for-a-bafta/)); design talk at [GDC 2020](https://gdcvault.com/play/1026925/The-Narrative-Innovation-Showcase-2020).
- **Why it sings at 2:** every puzzle is solved *in the conversation*, not on a screen; each device holds exactly half the picture, so a third person would have nothing to look at. Direct precedent for the two-phones question in the brief.

### Keep Talking and Nobody Explodes — actor/manual split under a countdown
- **Atom:** one side has the object, the other has the rules; time pressure forces lossy verbal compression.
- **Count:** 2+ — "One player takes the role of the bomb Defuser. All other players take the role of Experts" ([official how-to-play](https://www.bombmanual.com/how-to-play-pc.html)); "Going solo is not an option!" ([keeptalkinggame.com](https://keeptalkinggame.com/)).
- **Designer on why:** "The fun really lives between players… You have to make sure both sides have interesting, unique pieces of information to trade back and forth"; inputs are deliberately trivial so conversation, not execution, is the game — "we're making a communications-based party game, it just happens to have bombs in it" (Ben Kane, [Game Developer interview](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/finding-the-fun-in-bomb-defusal-with-i-keep-talking-and-nobody-explodes-i-)). Purest at exactly 2: one voice per side.

### We Were Here series — constrained voice channel as mechanic
- **Atom:** two-room asymmetry; "using only your voice and a walkie-talkie" ([Total Mayhem Games](https://totalmayhemgames.com/games/we-were-here/)) — and the walkie-talkie is half-duplex, so turn-taking and confirmation protocols become gameplay. Strictly 2P by authored design: each puzzle is two interlocking halves.

### The Past Within — the couple-phones structural cousin
- **Atom:** two eras, one player each; "Both players need to own a copy of the game and a way to communicate" ([Rusty Lake, Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1515210/The_Past_Within/)). Co-op only, cross-platform phones/PC. Not a word game — but the standout recent two-device couch co-op, proving the format.

### Briefly relevant
- **Fog of Love** — 2P-only relationship roleplay; hidden traits and secret Destinies inside one shared story. Designer Jacob Jaskov built it to play with his wife; inspired by *Before Sunrise*: "I wanted to create the feeling that you are talking to each other" ([Newsweek interview](https://www.newsweek.com/fog-love-creator-wants-games-every-part-relationships-1237799); [Floodgate page](https://floodgate.games/fogoflove)). Lesson: 2P-native = the dyad *is* the game. Not a word game.
- **Sherlock Holmes Consulting Detective** — **1–8** ([publisher metadata](https://www.amazon.com/Sherlock-Holmes-Consulting-Detective-Adventures/dp/2370990031)); scales to any count because there is *no hidden information between players* — everyone reads the same casebook and the deliberation aloud is the whole game. Press: "an astonishing coup for couples' gaming" ([Shut Up & Sit Down](https://www.shutupandsitdown.com/videos/review-sherlock-holmes-consulting-detective/), labeled press opinion). The inverse proof: remove info asymmetry and player count stops mattering.

### Digital 2P couch word games — the scan came back empty
No dedicated digital-native word/meaning game built for exactly two people on one couch was found. What exists: the official [codenames.game](https://codenames.game/) browser Duet (board-game port), the co-op-only [Wavelength app](https://www.wavelength.zone/app/faq), async duels (Words With Friends et al.), and couple question-decks with no game mechanics. The asymmetric-two-device genre (Tick Tock, The Past Within, We Were Here) has never been crossed with words/meaning as the material, and nothing anywhere has a constructor/referee seat. **The market gap is real.**

## Part B — beloved word games that officially fail or degrade at two

| Game | Official count | Missing crowd job at 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Taboo | 4+ ([Hasbro 1989 PDF](https://www.hasbro.com/common/instruct/Taboo_(1989).pdf)) | opponents staff the judge/buzzer |
| Codenames (base) | 4–8+ ([CGE](https://czechgames.com/en/codenames/)) | rival spymaster race |
| Just One | 3–7 ([Repos](https://www.rprod.com/en/games/just-one)) | ≥2 independent clue-writers to collide |
| Decrypto | 3–8 ([rulebook PDF](https://www.scorpionmasque.com/sites/scorpionmasque.com/files/decrypto_en_rules_20sep2019_1.pdf)) | the eavesdropper role |
| So Clover | 3–6 ([Repos](https://www.rprod.com/en/games/so-clover)) | plural guessing team deliberation |
| Monikers | 1st ed 4–16+; current "2–12+" ([CMYK](https://www.cmyk.games/products/monikers)) | teams + shared audience memory |
| Telestrations | 4 min ([The Op line](https://theop.games/collections/telestrations)) | chain length = the comedy |
| Balderdash | **2+** ([Mattel PDF](https://service.mattel.com/instruction_sheets/b9176-0910.pdf)) | the vote market (2P variant is a different game) |
| Dixit | 3–6 ([Libellud rules](https://cdn.svc.asmodee.net/production-libellud/uploads/2024/04/ODYSSEY_REFRESH_RULES_EN.pdf)) | "some but not all" needs ≥2 voters |
| Scattergories | **2–6** ([Hasbro 1988 PDF](https://www.hasbro.com/common/instruct/Scattergories.PDF)) | majority-vote adjudication |
| Wavelength (competitive) | 2–12 box; teams | opposing team for the left/right phase |
| The Chameleon | 3–8 ([Big Potato](https://bigpotato.com/products/the-chameleon)) | majority vote + hidden-role ambiguity |

**Taboo — the judge is staffed by opponents.** Official rules: "Team B's players… sit beside or behind Team A's Clue-giver so they can see the cards… Team B controls the buzzer"; "the members of the opposing team watch the Clue-giver and the cards" and buzz violations. Three simultaneous roles — giver, guesser (must NOT see the card), enforcer (must see it) — and at 2 the only other person is the guesser: watching and guessing are mutually exclusive. The whole penalty economy also pays a nonexistent opposing team. ([Hasbro 1989 rules PDF](https://www.hasbro.com/common/instruct/Taboo_(1989).pdf))

**Base Codenames — the rival mind becomes a clock.** Rulebook: "You need at least four players (two teams of two) for a standard game." The official back-page 2P variant: "If there are only two of you, you can play on the same team… You will try to get a high score against a simulated opponent"; the spymaster covers one enemy word per enemy turn, and win/lose becomes a score ladder (1: "Well, a win is a win, right?" … 8: "That is … difficult to believe."). The clue-guess loop survives; the tension source — being out-clued — does not. CGE's own cross-reference in that rulebook: "Codenames Duet is a cooperative version of Codenames specifically designed for small numbers of players." ([official rules PDF, archived](https://web.archive.org/web/2022id_/https://czechgames.com/files/rules/codenames-rules-en.pdf))

**Just One — cancellation cannot fire.** "Any identical clues are canceled" (including same-family words) is the entire decision: aim for the second-most-obvious clue while modeling the *other writers*. With one guesser there is exactly one writer, duplicates cannot occur, and the originality-vs-clarity risk disappears — vanilla one-clue charades. The official floor is proof: the 3–4 player variant gives each writer *two easels* ("two clues each") to preserve ≥2 independent writers. No 2P mode exists. ([official EN rules PDF](https://x.boardgamearena.net/data/rules/justone/JO_EN_Rules.pdf))

**Decrypto — three roles, two bodies.** "DECRYPTO pits two teams of 2 to 4 players against each other… Rules for 3 players can be found on page 10." Every clue serves two audiences: teammates (who see the keywords) and eavesdroppers (who see only clue history). The official 3P floor sacrifices one side's *entire communication game* to preserve interception (a lone "Interceptor" gives no clues) — showing which half the designer considers load-bearing. Designer origin: Bridge "gave me the idea to create a game about sending codes to your teammates, but running the risk of being intercepted by the enemy" ([Mojo Nation interview](https://www.mojo-nation.com/thomas-dagenais-lesperance-bridge-music-simplicity-aided-design-decrypto/)). At 2: a teammate means no interceptor (both win and loss conditions evaporate); an opponent means no one to talk to. ([rulebook PDF](https://www.scorpionmasque.com/sites/scorpionmasque.com/files/decrypto_en_rules_20sep2019_1.pdf))

**So Clover — see Part A**: the atom survives; the plural-guesser table layer (discussion, tie-break left of the Spectator) is what 3–6 buys.

**Monikers — crowd memory is the engine.** Team-vs-team over one shared deck across three escalating rounds; round 2's one-word and round 3's charades clues work only because everyone saw the cards described in round 1. First-edition official instructions were titled "For 4-16+ players" ([original PDF](https://buffalogames.com/product_images/uploaded_images/instructions/174-Monikers-Instructions_081816.pdf), now offline; title verified via search index); the current CMYK edition claims "2–12+" — resting on the co-op fallback: "If you're with a small group (4 or less), try playing cooperatively" ([CMYK page](https://www.cmyk.games/products/monikers); rulebook text via [transcription](https://www.rulespal.com/monikers/rulebook) matching the printed rules; community reverse-engineering of that mode: [BGG thread](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2282563), community signal).

**Telestrations — the chain is the comedy.** Sketch → guess → sketch, book passing until it returns for The Big Reveal; humor is error compounding through interior handoffs. At 2 a "chain" is one drawing and one guess — zero corruption steps; the reveal shows nothing the two didn't already witness. Official line minimum is 4 across the 6/8/12-player editions ([The Op](https://theop.games/collections/telestrations); [manual specs 4–12](https://www.manua.ls/usaopoly/telestrations-12-player-the-party-pack/manual)); friendly voting for favorites also presumes an audience.

**Balderdash — the smoking gun.** Officially "Players: 2 or more," and the sheet contains a full official 2-Player Game — but read what it swaps in: the Dasher writes ONE bluff *before seeing the real answer*, reads two options, and the guesser picks — 3 spaces for a fooled guesser, 2 for a right one. Gone: the pool of unattributed fakes, points-per-vote market ("1 point… for every vote his/her answer earned"), vote-splitting ambiguity. With one opponent the guesser knows the only fake is theirs; Mattel quietly shipped a different (binary quiz) game on the same components — and had to add the write-blind constraint to compensate for process-of-elimination. ([Mattel B9176 instruction PDF](https://service.mattel.com/instruction_sheets/b9176-0910.pdf), verified directly)

**Dixit — the storyteller mathematically cannot score.** Scoring is "some but not all": "if all players have found the storyteller's image, or if none have found it, then the storyteller doesn't score." With one voter the outcome set is {all, none} — the storyteller scores zero every turn, forever; decoy bonuses (votes for YOUR card) also need ≥2 voters to form a market. Libellud patched exactly to 3 (a 3-player variant with extra submitted cards) and stopped. A BGG "Dixit for 2 players variants collection" exists precisely because official scoring can't function ([community signal](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/1027565)). ([official Odyssey rules PDF](https://cdn.svc.asmodee.net/production-libellud/uploads/2024/04/ODYSSEY_REFRESH_RULES_EN.pdf))

**Scattergories — your only opponent is your only judge.** Officially "FOR 2 TO 6 PLAYERS," but adjudication is by vote: "all players… vote on whether the answer is acceptable… Majority rules. In the case of a tie, the challenged player's vote does not count." At 2 every challenge is a 1–1 tie resolved by striking the *challenged* player's vote — the challenger unilaterally wins every dispute, holding formal veto over your creative answers. And uniqueness scoring ("score if nobody matched you") is calibrated for crowd collisions; with one opponent nearly everything scores. Parallel solitaire plus a broken court. ([Hasbro 1988 rules PDF](https://www.hasbro.com/common/instruct/Scattergories.PDF))

**Briefly:** *Fictionary* (folk ancestor of Balderdash) has the same collapse — one bluff vs. one truth is a coin-flip, no vote market ([rules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fictionary)). *The Chameleon* patches to exactly 3 ("the Chameleon gets two guesses if caught") and stops; at 2, hidden-role certainty plus no majority vote ([Big Potato page](https://bigpotato.com/products/the-chameleon)).

### The crowd's six jobs (what exactly is missing at two)
Traced across the official rules above, "needs a group" decomposes into six distinct load-bearing functions:
1. **Opponent-as-judge** — enforcement staffed by the rival team (Taboo's buzzer; Scattergories' challenge vote).
2. **Independent co-writers** — multiple minds that can collide (Just One's cancellation).
3. **Vote market / probability field** — enough voters that bluffs can split them (Balderdash, Fictionary, Dixit's some-but-not-all, Chameleon).
4. **Chain length** — interior handoffs that compound error into comedy (Telestrations).
5. **Eavesdropper** — a third role listening to the open channel (Decrypto).
6. **Rival team + audience memory** — race pressure and shared context (base Codenames, Monikers, Wavelength's left/right phase).

### The official patch playbook (how publishers themselves fix two)
- **Simulate the opponent as a clock** — base Codenames' dummy team auto-reveals one word per turn.
- **Redesign both seats as halves of one puzzle** — Codenames Duet's overlapping double-sided key (the only purpose-built-for-2 game in the set).
- **Add an artificial seat with public info and a no-discussion rule** — The Crew's JARVIS/Tonoja, Letter Jam's nonplayer stands, and industry-wide Automa bots.
- **Swap head-to-head for a graded co-op score ladder** — Wavelength co-op, Medium 2P ("just play for fun"), Monikers' co-op fallback, Codenames 2P scoring table.
- **Ship a different game on the same components** — Balderdash's official 2-Player Game.
- **Patch down to exactly 3 and stop** — Decrypto's Interceptor, Dixit's 3P variant, Just One's dual easels, Chameleon's two-guess rule. Three is the industry's hard floor wherever a judging/voting/eavesdropping mind is required.

## Signals for our design (all inference)

Everything below is our inference from the evidence above, for a couple + LLM referee on two phones (couch, cooperative lean, Melissa's clever-aha target).

1. **The LLM's seat is precisely the unstaffable crowd jobs.** Every job cardboard successfully automates is *mechanical* — JARVIS's public cards, Letter Jam's letter stacks, Codenames' auto-reveal clock, Automa decks. Every job left unfixed at 2 (Taboo's judge, Just One's co-writer, Dixit/Balderdash's vote field, Decrypto's eavesdropper, So Clover's second guesser) requires a seat that *understands free language*. That is the exact boundary of the LLM's claim — it staffs the seats no component ever could, which is why these games sat broken at 2 for decades.
2. **Follow the JARVIS design rules for the third seat:** the artificial player's information should be public or semi-public, its rulings should not add a hidden mind between the humans, and it must never absorb the couple's conversation — the humans' channel stays the scarce, precious thing. (KTaNE's designer says the quiet part: the fun "lives between players." The LLM referees the talk; it must not become the talk.)
3. **Keep both humans symmetric clue-givers.** Duet's core fix was making both players spymaster-and-guesser at once; base Codenames' 2P variant shows how thin a fixed giver/guesser split feels. Tick Tock does the same digitally — both get a screen, both get half the picture. Avoid any mode where one person is permanently "it."
4. **Cooperative with a graded, flavor-texted score ladder** — every single official 2P conversion in this research abandons head-to-head for a co-op ladder (Wavelength's "Galaxy brain," Codenames' "difficult to believe," Duet's mission map). Medium's "just play for fun" is the cautionary tale: without a ladder, victory has no texture. Duet's mission map (varying turns/mistakes across cities) is a ready-made template for the brief's legacy/campaign direction.
5. **Decoys are the misdirection engine that already works at 2.** So Clover's blind-drawn decoy card is misdirection *without a judge*; Duet's overlapping keys make your safe word my assassin. This is Melissa's savored red-herring pleasure, and it's the one crowd-free trick in the group games. An LLM decoy-master generalizes it: infinite boards where the decoys are crafted, not drawn — with the So Clover lesson that decoys chosen blind are automatically fair, so a generated-before-play decoy is more trustworthy than one improvised mid-game.
6. **Two phones + voice is a proven format with an empty niche.** Tick Tock and The Past Within prove couples will play two-device games where each screen holds half the picture and the voice channel is the only network; Duet's key-card stand is the same idea in cardboard. Nobody has crossed that format with words/meaning — and nothing digital anywhere has a constructor/referee seat. The market gap the thesis targets is confirmed real, not crowded.
7. **Nearest-atom shortlist for the brief's candidates:** Clover Duet inherits a 2P-coherent atom (clue-binding pairs) and needs the LLM only as second-guesser/decoy-master/tie-breaker — lowest risk. Taboo for Two needs the LLM as full judge (the hardest unstaffed seat, and the purest test of the thesis). Mind-Meld Bridges is Medium's convergence atom, already dyad-native — the LLM adds graded semantic-distance scoring where Medium could only offer binary match/miss. A Decrypto-descendant would cast the LLM as eavesdropper — the couple encodes past a machine that provably *reads* — an unexplored inversion worth prototyping.
8. **Hanabi's caveat generalizes:** atoms that scale with the number of *observers* (triangulating on third parties' exchanges) will always feel thin at 2 regardless of refereeing. Prefer atoms whose information geometry is dyadic (calibration, convergence, pair-binding, split-screens) over ones that are merely dyad-legal.
