# Design brief — the meaning game

Status: group-play grilling closed 2026-09-04 (ADR-0010); next is `/to-spec` on `.scratch/group-play/`. Last updated 2026-09-04.

## Vision

A game that plays with meaning. The bar, above everything else: **fun** — operationalized as **"a game my wife likes."**

## Players

- **My wife** — the target player. Loves So Clover and NYT Connections (savors the red-herring misdirection and narrates the clever traps afterward), Boggle (family history), crosswords. Likes Codenames, Taboo, Telestrations, Monikers, Tick Tock: A Tale for Two (finished recently, played with Jacob), probably Scrabble; Wordle to a lesser extent. Plays a word/meaning game roughly daily on her phone (Pixel 10 Pro XL, Android). Doesn't play humorous games alone — wit belongs in the content; clever-aha is the primary fun.
- **Jacob** — builder, and co-player if the two-player direction wins.

## The confirmed pattern

Her favorite games are all *one word's meaning stretched to cover several things at once*, spiced with **fair misdirection**. And the most-loved tier — So Clover, Codenames, Taboo, Monikers, Telestrations, Tick Tock — is entirely **communication games**: meaning passed between minds. The solo puzzles (Connections, crosswords, Boggle) are her alone-games; the loved-most tier is the together-tier.

## Settled decisions

- **Fun target**: clever-aha first; wit woven into the content, not slapstick gameplay.
- **Platform**: mobile-first web app, judged purely on fun grounds — maximum mechanic-experiments per hour, and a URL opens instantly on my wife's Pixel. A game engine (Godot/Bevy/etc.) gets adopted only if the chosen mechanic demands physical juice; unfamiliarity will not count against it.
- **Tech selection principle**: choices are judged only by the fun of the result and speed of iterating toward it — never by what Jacob already knows.
- **LLM / agents at runtime**: allowed and actively interesting — the affordances of non-deterministic language technology in games are under-explored — but **optional, not a mandate**. An LLM must earn its place by enabling something otherwise impossible.
- **Scope**: a household toy until fun is proven; maybe a public thing (b) or product (c) after.
- **Two-player** (decided 2026-08-31): designed for Jacob and his wife playing *together* — same couch, synchronous, evenings. No workday async; Jacob protects daytime focus. Her solo slots are saturated; the couple slot has been vacant since they finished Tick Tock. A solo mode can come later (the LLM understudies Jacob's seat).
- **Design thesis for the machine** (revised 2026-08-31 — see ADR-0001): **the machine holds no player seat.** If a human could do a role better, the role stays human, or the design waits for more humans. The machine takes only superhuman jobs: nightly author of boards for an audience of two, steward of the Chronicle, instant and consistent meter of meaning-distance — the couple's live-in game designer, scribe, and instrument. (This supersedes the earlier "third seat makes group games two-player" framing; the research's six crowd jobs remain a map of what *not* to automate when a person would relish the job.)
- **Table hardware**: two phones as private screens; a laptop as an optional shared public screen is open creative space (reveal theater, Chronicle recaps) — private/public information display is a design axis to exploit.
- **Stance**: cooperative versus the game with graded score ladders. My wife also enjoys competitive Boggle, so a light competitive garnish may return in later modes.
- **Legacy**: light memory in the prototype, architected so campaign structure is reachable; the artifact is the **Chronicle** (see CONTEXT.md), visible and player-editable per design law 5.
- **Session atom**: a **Page** (decided 2026-09-01) — one self-contained unit of play that ends cleanly in ~10–15 minutes and chains ("one more?" is a single tap); every Page is written into the Chronicle. The vocabulary is Page → Chapter → Chronicle (see CONTEXT.md). No streaks, no obligation mechanics.
- **Chronicle storage** (decided 2026-09-01, ADR-0003): Postgres via Ecto — append-only event tables (Pages, rulings, overrules, player edits; versions are the log itself, no in-game git), the Page document as JSONB; already running on the server and reachable from Docker containers, docker-compose locally. Views are computed, never stored: the LiveView book page for players, a Steward-composed digest for in-game models, and an on-demand Markdown render (`mix chronicle.export` or `GET /chronicle.md`) for humans and coding agents. pgvector recall over past Pages is a post-couch-night feature.
- **Referee posture** (closed 2026-09-01): grades not gavels; reasoning always shown; the couple can jointly overrule anything, and overrules are recorded in the Chronicle as precedent.
- **Stack** (decided 2026-09-01, ADR-0002): Phoenix / Elixir / LiveView, hosted on stephens.page. Chosen for rooms, server-authoritative state, the private/public split, and less client code — and because Jacob wants to learn Elixir. Kill criterion: three evenings to a working two-phone room with rejoin, else TypeScript + Socket.IO.
- **Model policy, v1** (fun regardless of cost; **measured** by the Steward prototype on 2026-09-02 — primary source `prototype/keeper/REPORT.md` on branch `prototype/keeper`, spend ≈ $14): Author `claude-fable-5-1` at max effort **holds for Mind-Meld** ($0.15–0.32 and 43–87 s per Page; all 12 blind A/B Pages schema-valid and clue-legal on the first attempt; Fable's Pages beat GPT-5.6 Sol's on narratable wit). Clover at `max` costs ~8 min / $1.80 per Page (retired with clover; `high` was legal and good at ~2 min / $0.35; a token-cap overrun is terminal, never retried). Scribe `claude-fable-5-1` writes valid but long recaps — the Vale gate is needed as planned. **Assayer**: the GLM 5.3-Flash contingency is **met** from the VPS (314 ms P50 first token, 961 ms P95 whole verdict) but GLM is the noisiest candidate (repeat range up to 35 points, one 85-point ordering swing); **Gemini 3.8 Flash at low effort is the new front-runner** — GLM-class latency, Opus-class repeatability, grading the 40–75 band 10–15 points more generously than the Anthropic models (a constant offset, not noise); Sonnet 5 low is the fairest that fits the beat (2 s P50); Opus 5 low misses the P50 rule by ~0.1 s. Kimi K3 still to bench (Together key pending). Hedging: two identical requests cut the fast tier's P95 (GLM 1.52 → 1.17 s) and do nothing for Opus. All via API keys (consumer subscriptions don't license app use). Per-role model + effort config behind the Steward; verdict cache keyed by model + prompt version + normalized inputs, both orderings → one key, an overrule writes precedent into the same key.
- **A/B testing is a v1 feature in its own right** — Jacob wants the practice for professional work: Page-level randomized assignment, blinded variant labels, stratification by Chapter, every outcome logged to the Chronicle, a qualitative readout (n is two people, so streaks and preferences, not p-values).
- **Code shape principle**: few deep modules and boring data. The game's rules should fit in one readable file — Karpathy-style minimalism reached *through* deep-module design, not against it; framework scaffolding stays generated and thin.
- **Steward** (decided 2026-09-04, ADR-0009): the one module through which the game talks to language models — see CONTEXT.md. Vale's AiTells package is adopted for the Scribe gate.
- **Git conventions** (2026-09-01): design docs commit directly to `master`; `master` stays deployable (the Docker release builds from it); the prototype lives on `prototype/<name>` and is never merged; later feature work on short-lived branches with `/code-review` before merge, GitHub PRs optional.
- **Assayer** (named 2026-09-01): the grading role — measures closeness and clue legality at the reveal, shows its reasoning, never rules; a nod to my wife's daily lab assays. See CONTEXT.md.
- **Couch-night hosting** (decided 2026-09-02): the prototype runs on **stephens.page from the start**, not over the home LAN — every evening then measures the real VPS round-trips that the reveal beat has to absorb, and the deploy loop is exercised long before couch night. Server shell access: `ssh jacob@stephens.page`. A secure context also means the Screen Wake Lock API is available, so phones need no timeout fiddling.
- **Prototype staging** (decided 2026-09-02): Stage 0 first — a single throwaway HTML file driving both Page machines with a fake Author and Assayer (branch `prototype/stage0-page-machines`) — before the Phoenix playable slice, so the rules are felt before any plumbing exists.
- **Deployment** (decided 2026-09-01): Docker release (`mix phx.gen.release --docker`) so the identical image runs on the Mac and on the server; behind an Apache 2.4.58 vhost (`ProxyPass "/" "http://127.0.0.1:<port>/" upgrade=websocket`, certbot for TLS) matching Jacob's existing pattern of localhost-proxied apps. Elixir/Erlang via mise on the Mac for development.
- **Rooms and identity** (decided 2026-09-02, ADR-0007): the game is not essentially two-player — Mind-Meld generalizes Jackbox-style to a group. Rooms and the Game are **N-seat from the first line of code, two by default**; Stage 1's UI stays two phones plus the stage. **Joining takes only a name**; a seat holds a stable id and a device token; an **Account exists only to claim a seat's history afterwards** for per-person insights. Magic-link email first, Google sign-in later; accounts are post-couch-night. Chronicle rows reference seats, never accounts.
- **Group play** (decided 2026-09-04, ADR-0010; prompted by an evening with another couple, Bram and Esme): **one Verdict per Attempt** — the Assayer grades every Bridge as a set, Closeness meaning convergence (three tight words and a stray read as close), and names the two furthest-apart Bridges in the same Verdict; those two carry forward. The Page melds when that one number reaches the meld band. At two Seats nothing changes. Not chosen: the pairwise meld map ADR-0007 sketched (kept as an idea for a stage that exists) and couple-versus-couple (filed). **Rooms get a Code**: a `/new` page takes a player count (two to six), the Scribe names the Room with a four-letter dictionary word that nods to the Rooms the creator's phone already holds Seats in (Gemini 3.8 Flash, so nobody waits; random word when there is no history or the model is slow), and the creator joins by name like everyone else. The bare URL becomes a landing page — Code box, New Room, one-tap rejoin links — and an unknown Code is a 404. Player-facing copy says "Room". **Kept as they are**: unanimity of the Room to overrule; one shared Precedent across Rooms; one Chronicle per Room; one fairness tap per Seat per Attempt, rows kept with their Bridge count; both orderings averaged; the seat cap of six, now about the phone layout. The reveal on a phone is every Bridge, the number and band, the quip, the reasoning, then the taps — the stage is optional. Follow-ups filed under `.scratch/group-play/issues/` and `.scratch/scribe/issues/`. No deadline: building it well outranked playing it that night.
- **Legacy direction** (interest declared; depth undecided): sessions write persistent files that shape future games — Pandemic Legacy energy. The game remembers the couple's history and plays it back.

## Design laws (from research)

Distilled from `docs/research/llm-in-word-games.md` (prior art on LLM judges/referees/game-masters in word games; each law cites primary sources there):

1. **Consistency beats accuracy.** Players forgive wrong rulings, never self-contradicting ones. Same inputs → same ruling, forever; cache every verdict.
2. **Grade, don't gavel.** Comparative/graded feedback (rank, closeness) defuses resentment; binary verdicts survive only at joke stakes.
3. **Humans hold final authority.** Appeals are shipped precedent; a joint player overrule is legitimate and becomes Chronicle lore.
4. **Pre-generate and rank, don't live-generate.** Boards/decoys made ahead and ranked — turns generation problems into ranking problems and kills mid-play latency.
5. **Memory is a visible, player-editable artifact.** Silent forgetting and opaque rulings are named trust-killers; the Chronicle should be readable and correctable by the players.

From `docs/research/two-player-communication-mechanics.md` (why group word games fail at two, per official rules and designers' own words):

6. **The crowd does six nameable jobs**: opponent-as-judge, independent co-writers, vote market, chain length, eavesdropper, rival-team race. "Third seat" = staffing whichever of these the chosen mechanic needs.
7. **The boundary is free language.** Publishers successfully staff *mechanical* seats with dummies and bots (The Crew's JARVIS, Letter Jam's stands, Automa); every seat that must *understand free language* was left broken at two or given a hard 3-player floor. That unstaffable seat is precisely the LLM's claim.
8. **At two, go co-op with a graded score ladder.** Every official 2P conversion did (Wavelength co-op, Medium, Codenames' scoring table, Monikers' fallback); Codenames Duet's mission map is a ready-made legacy hook.
9. **Misdirection survives without a rival team** two proven ways: symmetric clue-givers with overlapping keys (Codenames Duet — designed by Scot Eaton to play with his wife), or blind decoys (So Clover). Both fit a couple.

From `docs/research/model-selection-for-play.md` (leaderboard methodology, HCI latency evidence, judge-quality literature):

10. **The reveal is a fixed beat, not a wait.** Fire the Meter the instant the second player commits; a fixed ~2.5 s reveal beat absorbs the p50 verdict (Shneiderman licenses slowing fast responses to keep variability under ±50%); p95 ≤ 4 s with visible activity; provisional result at 8 s; never past 10 s. Shorten the beat for harsh grades (the labor illusion backfires on bad news).
11. **Nothing is deterministic at any tier** — 1,000 temperature-0 calls yielded 80 unique completions. The verdict cache is the consistency mechanism; hedged requests must be *identical* requests to *one* model, never different models.
12. **Grade both orderings in parallel** (position bias is the tier-linked judge failure) and read the leaderboard at the effort row you'd actually run: its Latency column already includes thinking time. Meter rule of thumb: `Latency + 60 ÷ Speed ≤ 2.5 s`. The Intelligence Index contains no semantic-judgment, word-association, or puzzle-construction evals — it is a shortlist, never a decision.
13. **Authors must be frontier-tier** — small models fail on rule/format-following (invalid clues), while frontier similarity ratings match or beat human inter-annotator agreement. Where the answer space allows, the Author pre-grades so the live Meter is a lookup.

From `docs/research/deterministic-guardrails.md` (Vale, Elixir stemming/wordlists, provider schema guarantees):

14. **Never send a model to do a linter's job.** Every role's output passes a deterministic gate: a schema validator has the last word (providers guarantee shape unevenly — Gemini says "always validate"); Author clues pass Elixir legality filters (regex, ENABLE/SCOWL wordlists, stem + inflection-table root check, profanity list) with reject-and-regenerate; Scribe prose passes a Vale gate (custom Scribe style + write-good + proselint + AiTells) with one model repair pass. Shell out only for Vale; everything else lives in Elixir.

Validated gap, both files agreeing: no prior art combines LLM referee + two-player meaning game + legacy memory — and no digital-native two-player couch word game exists at all (two-device voice-linked co-op is proven by Tick Tock / The Past Within, but never crossed with words).

## Open questions

- **Stage 0 verdict (Jacob, 2026-09-02)** — the throwaway logic prototype (branch `prototype/stage0-page-machines`) was clicked through solo on the laptop:
  - **Mind-Meld won on fun, and the graded number is the reason.** A 24/100 closeness rating drew "a pang of smirk" — the Assayer's score landed as a *punchline*, not as a referee's ruling, from a stub Assayer with a toy heuristic. Read against ADR-0001: the machine's superhuman job (instant, consistent, graded meter of meaning-distance) is not merely infrastructure, it is the **comedy delivery mechanism**. Stage 1 should build the reveal as a performance — ladder flavor text and the reasoning line are load-bearing fun, not chrome.
  - **Nuance on design law 10**: the law says shorten the beat for harsh grades, because the labor illusion backfires on bad news. Here the *harsh* grade was the fun. Bad news in a co-op meaning game may be the joke rather than the sting — worth testing with my wife before shortening anything.
  - **Chronicle Clover left Jacob wanting the physical cards.** So Clover's pleasure is spatial — you push cards around and *watch* the relationships resolve. A list of four dropdowns kills it. Simulable (drag, rotation, a real clover layout), but that is significant UI work; cardboard won before reaching the couch. This was the smell that led to retiring it unplayed on 2026-09-02 rather than building the spatial layout.
  - Caveat on both: one player, not the target player.
- **Stage 1 scope narrowed (decided 2026-09-02)**: the Phoenix playable slice builds **Mind-Meld only**. It won Stage 0 on fun, and it needs no card layout — two anchors, two text inputs, one graded reveal — so the build is mostly room/seat/Chronicle plumbing and the ADR-0002 three-evening kill criterion gets a fair test. **Chronicle Clover was retired, unplayed, on 2026-09-02**; the prototype stays on `prototype/stage0-page-machines` as a primary source, but is no longer a live option or parked candidate. My wife never played it: it lost at Stage 0 on UI feel before reaching the couch. The product and the game are one object. My wife does not click Stage 0 first: her first contact is the working two-phone game, not a stub with nonsense clues.
- **Prototype pair superseded (2026-09-02)**: the earlier plan (2026-09-01) for an A/B couch night comparing Chronicle Clover and Mind-Meld was superseded when the clover candidate was retired unplayed on 2026-09-02. The A/B harness instead tests bait-scored Pages against plain ones (ADR-0004) within Mind-Meld. Adaptive Taboo stays benched.
- **Chapters and gives** (adopted as a direction, 2026-09-01): on-request exploration of a meaning-space, at two commitment levels — a deliberate themed journey (e.g. words of suburban family life, of Children's Hospital of Philadelphia, of the world St. Thérèse grew up in, of Newman's vein) or an improv-style instant give (Whose Line energy: name a setting, and tonight's play is suddenly set there). This is the Author's signature power: no publisher can print a deck for a meaning-space you name at 9pm.
- **House-rules as precedent** (Nomic-inspired, bounded): joint overrules and standing preferences accrete in the Chronicle as the couple's house-law, so their copy of the game slowly becomes *theirs*. Full Nomic (rule-amendment as the game) deliberately waits for three-plus humans; 1000-Blank-White-Cards-style player authorship is the direction — absorbed by the machine, not simulated by it.
- **Naming, parked until after couch night**: the game's real name (clover was retired unplayed on 2026-09-02, so there is only one game to name); the umbrella term for the machine's roles is settled as Steward (ADR-0009). The session atom is settled as Page.
- **Clue-legality library**: build in Elixir (stem + prefix + inflection-table root check, ENABLE/SCOWL wordlists, profanity list; the grading model as cached fallback for the long tail). The need is small and not a reason to revisit ADR-0002; a candidate to extract as an open-source hex package once the prototype proves it.
- **Deterministic guardrails**: research landed (design law 14; per-role pipelines in docs/research/deterministic-guardrails.md). Adopt in the cheapest form — schema validation everywhere, the clue-legality filter for the Author — and add the Vale gate (Scribe style + AiTells) once the Scribe exists. Measured by the Steward prototype: 0 schema failures in ~2,900 JSON responses, yet the deterministic checks a schema cannot express (exactly four disjoint clovers, clue legality, cap overruns) were essential.
- **Steward prototype verdicts** (2026-09-02, branch `prototype/keeper`, `REPORT.md`): all six questions answered — Author quality yes; Assayer budget met from the VPS; nothing is deterministic (law 11 with numbers); Opus's grades read fairest, GLM erratic in the middle band, Gemini stable but generous; Elixir friction low (a 30-line SSE parser covers six providers, `jsv` clean — ADR-0002 stands); Fable 5.1 behaves as documented from raw HTTP (a refusal is HTTP 200 + `stop_reason: "refusal"` + a category; `fallbacks: "default"` was accepted but does not cover `reasoning_extraction`; no Author call was ever refused). Contract additions proposed: decoys carry `targets_pair` and the Author's rationale; Mind-Meld Pages carry `likely_bridges` and `why_these` (a usable pre-graded table, per law 13); verdicts carry `forward`, `reverse`, `reasoning_reverse`, `model`, `prompt_version`, `timings`, `cost_usd`; Pages carry `meta` (author model, prompt version, attempts, cost).
- **Return-leg decisions (2026-09-02)** — the questions both prototypes raised, closed:
  - **Misdirection**: bait bridges (ADR-0004), shipped as the A/B harness's first experiment — bait-scored Pages vs. plain.
  - **Author's job in Mind-Meld**: anchors, the bait, at least two deep bridges, `likely_bridges` + `why_these` as the trap's narration, Chapter walks. **The live joke belongs to the Assayer** — Gemini 3.8 Flash quips on the fly, naming the players' actual bridges (Jacob's call over Author-prewritten band scripts, which stay as fallback and A/B arm).
  - **Live Assayer**: Gemini 3.8 Flash at low effort, calibrated by the couple's ~100-pair labelled eval (ADR-0005). Building that labelled set is a couch task and the first A/B artifact.
  - **Precedent**: the grade shown is the precedent; second opinions are annotations; only a joint overrule rewrites (ADR-0006).
  - **Reveal beat**: fixed 2.5 s for every grade in v1 — harsh grades here are shared punchlines, not personal stings; law 10's shortening rule is deferred until my wife has played.
  - **Placeholders kept as named config, not design**: meld threshold 75, attempt cap 5 (tries per Page before it ends unmelded), 0–100 scale, five ladder bands (miss / adjacent / near / meld / deep meld — wording is Assayer/Author output, boundaries set by calibration), and Medium's carry-forward rule (failed bridges become the next anchors) kept for the converging arc.
  - **Contracts, hedging, fallback**: the Steward prototype's contract additions accepted; hedging = two identical requests for the fast tier only; the refusal-fallback header stays on the Author, logged, never depended on.
  - **Glossary**: Anchors, Bridge, Meld, Bait, Precedent added to CONTEXT.md.
- **Group play**: closed 2026-09-04 — one Verdict per Attempt (ADR-0010, see Settled decisions).
- **Naming**: Jacob wants a dedicated session for the game's name and alternatives; parked, not forgotten. Ticketed 2026-09-03 as `.scratch/naming/issues/01-the-games-real-name.md` (`ready-for-human`, decision blocked on couch night) — "Mind-Meld" is a working label (Chronicle Clover having been retired unplayed on 2026-09-02), and a shortlist is on the ticket. **Availability answered** (`docs/research/name-mind-meld-availability.md`): the name is taken — a **live US Class 28 registration** (Reg. 7540401, Golden Bear Products Ltd.) recites "party games involving word challenges" and is backed by a box in retail, and "Mind Meld" turns out to be the **generic name of this game type** (a standard improv warm-up that predates every product found), which is why four unrelated games already carry it. Paramount holds nothing and never has, and mechanics are outside copyright — both feared risks were empty. Verdict: **fine as the working title** (private couch use is not use in trade; keep the deploy `noindex`), **retire it as a shipped name**. Cleanest alternatives on the register are **Near Enough** and **Deep Meld** (zero records in any class); drop **Halfway**.
- **Post-couch-night Postgres roadmap** (deliberately out of the prototype): **Oban** (Postgres-native job queue, signals workers via LISTEN/NOTIFY) for overnight Author pre-generation, post-play Scribe runs, and Assayer benchmarks; **pgvector** for personal-best closeness ladders ("third-closest you've ever landed") and thematic callbacks when a Give is semantically near past Pages; embedding cosine as a deterministic closeness instrument to interleave against the Assayer; embeddings via Voyage/OpenAI/Google or locally with Bumblebee. JSONB for the Page document is used from the prototype onward.
- Deprioritized: outward sharing/result cards — couch play removes the original need; revisit only if the game goes public.
