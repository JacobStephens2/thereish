# 01: The game's real name

> *This was generated by AI during triage.*

**Category:** enhancement
**Status:** ready-for-human
**Blocked by:** couch night (not a ticket — see "Why this waits")

**What to decide:** What the game is actually called. "Mind-Meld" is a working label, never a chosen name, and it has been carrying the whole project — a tracker directory, a spec, three ADRs and two research notes — as though it were settled.

## Why this is open

The design brief parks naming twice, and has never unparked it:

- *"**Naming, parked until after couch night**: both games' real names; the umbrella term for the machine's roles is settled as Steward (ADR-0009). The session atom is settled as Page."*
- *"**Naming**: Jacob wants a dedicated session for the game's name and alternatives; parked, not forgotten."*

"Mind-Meld" sits on exactly the same footing as its A/B partner, which the brief flags explicitly: *"the clover candidate (working label "Chronicle Clover" — **deliberately *not* a real name**; too close to So Clover)"*. Both were placeholders. The clover label got a collision note; Mind-Meld never got the same scrutiny.

The taste problem, stated before the availability research ran and unchanged by it: **it names the mechanic, not the joke.** Stage 0's finding was that the *harsh grade is the punchline* — a 24/100 drew "a pang of smirk". "Mind-Meld" names the win condition. The evening's actual pleasure is the miss.

## Findings: the name is taken (2026-09-03)

Full note at `docs/research/name-mind-meld-availability.md`, primary sources throughout. Four things changed the picture:

1. **A live US Class 28 registration sits exactly on top of this project.** Reg. **7540401**, *MIND MELD*, **Golden Bear Products Ltd.** (UK), registered 2024-10-22, reciting "party games involving word challenges… board games featuring word challenges". Backed by a box currently in retail; no maintenance window until 2029. Not a dormant paper mark. Cisco separately holds a live *MINDMELD* in Class 9 — the class a game app would need.
2. **Paramount holds nothing, and never has.** Zero CBS/Paramount/Viacom marks contain "MELD" anywhere on the register, and the phrase has never been opposed or cancelled by anyone. The Star Trek fear was empty. So was the mechanic fear: game mechanics are categorically outside copyright (Copyright Office Compendium §313.3(A), Circular 33).
3. **"Mind Meld" is the generic name of this game type, not a name for it.** It is a standard improv-theatre warm-up — count to three, converge on a connecting word, repeat until you match — documented by Hoopla Impro and predating every product found. That is this project's loop in one sentence. Hence four independent arrivals at the name: Golden Bear's box, *MindMeld: The Party Game* (TMI Games, 2023, shipping our exact mechanic), a live Google Play word puzzle, and an itch.io browser game that is this project in miniature. Calling it Mind Meld is like calling it *Charades*: it names the category, buys no distinctiveness, and guarantees the game is permanently invisible behind four other things in any search box.
4. **Two premises were wrong, and both came from the research prompt rather than from these docs.** *Medium* is a **Greater Than Games** title (2019), not Spin Master — `docs/research/two-player-communication-mechanics.md` already had this right. And there is no Jimmy Fallon "Mind Meld" segment; three independent checks came back negative. The nearest real segment is "You Read My Mind."

**Verdict: keep it as the working title, retire it as a shipped name.** A private game on `stephens.page` with no listing and no promotion is not use in the ordinary course of trade; exposure is nil, and renaming before couch night would spend attention on the one question that cannot be answered until Melissa has played. Shipping fails on four independent axes — trademark, app-store collision, distinctiveness, and the taste argument above. Two cheap hygiene measures while the working title stands: keep the deployment `noindex`, and keep the visual identity clear of Star Trek livery.

## Why this waits

The name should come from what the game turns out to feel like, and as of this ticket nobody has played it. The one data point — Stage 0 — is a solo click-through on a laptop by the player who is not the target player, against a stub Assayer with a toy heuristic.

So: the legwork lands now, the decision lands after Melissa has played a Page. Success on this ticket is the same as success everywhere else on this project — Melissa likes it.

## Why this is not delegable

Naming is taste, measured against one person's taste. An agent can generate candidates and clear them for collisions; it cannot pick. The generative and clearing halves are done or doable now; the pick is Jacob and Melissa's, at the couch.

## Rename cost, measured at the time of writing

17 occurrences of "Mind-Meld", all in documentation — `CONTEXT.md`, the design brief, the spec, ADRs 0004 / 0005 / 0007, and two research notes. **Zero in `lib/`.** The OTP app is `:meaning_game` / `MeaningGame`, an umbrella name that survives any rename.

The only structural cost is the tracker directory and its ticket paths. Today the rename is a `sed` and a `git mv`. Once the build lands it becomes module names, routes, and the Chronicle's own player-visible prose — and the Chronicle is append-only, so early Pages would keep the old name forever.

As of 2026-09-03 the in-flight deploy work also bakes the name into infrastructure: `bin/deploy` and `deploy/meld.stephens.page.conf` target the subdomain **meld.stephens.page**, and the vhost, log paths and certbot cert all carry it. That is a DNS record, an Apache vhost and a TLS cert to redo, not just a `sed` — still small, but it is the first non-documentation cost this decision has picked up, and it appeared within a day of the ticket.

**This is the cheapest this decision will ever be.** That is the argument for not letting it drift much past couch night.

## Shortlist to react to

Generated from the glossary the project already built and from the Stage 0 finding. The **register half** of clearance is already done for all eight (research note §6, USPTO Classes 9/28/41, zero-hit queries control-tested against near neighbours so a zero is provably real, not a broken query). Still missing for every candidate: common-law use, non-US registers, and app-store listings — only Steam and BGG were checked for existing products. None is cleared on the strength of that table alone.

Domains are not a differentiator: all eight `.com`s are registered, all eight `.game`s are free.

**Naming the miss** (follows the Stage 0 finding — the grade is the punchline):

- **Near Enough** — wry, undersold, exactly the register of a 24/100 landing as a joke. **The cleanest of the eight on the register: zero USPTO records in any class, in any status; nothing on Steam or BGG.** The happy accident is that the register agrees with the taste argument. Weak/suggestive marks are harder to *enforce*, but nothing blocks it.
- **Betwixt** — the Bridge is literally the word betwixt two Anchors. Archaic, warm, distinctive. **Re-check rather than assume:** Class 28 is clear, but *DUNGEON BETWIXT* was filed in Classes 9 and 41 on 2026-07-08 and is live and unexamined, and a 2019 board game called *Betwixt and Between* already sits in the same 1–4-player space.

**Naming the couple**:

- **Two Heads** — from the spec's own line, *"one mind, two heads"*. Nothing live on the register. Weak as a distinguisher though: a common idiom, so the risk is descriptiveness rather than conflict. Scales awkwardly to the N-seat rooms ADR-0007 committed to.
- **The Word Between Us** — intimate, exact about the mechanic. The phrase itself returns zero records, but the "___ BETWEEN US" family is busy and includes two live Class 28 games, so expect a crowded-field examiner search. Long.
- ~~**Halfway** / **Meet Me Halfway**~~ — **drop Halfway outright**: two Steam games are literally titled *Halfway*, the worst existing-product collision of the eight. *Meet Me Halfway* survives but is phonetically identical to a live *MEAT ME HALFWAY* and is a well-known song title, so it risks a failure-to-function refusal.

**Naming the grade**:

- **Assay** — the Assayer is already a deliberate nod to Melissa's daily lab assays. Short, odd, and clear in Classes 28 and 41. But Class 9 is full of live *ASSAY* marks (all biotech), so a game-app filing lands in a crowded class, and it reads as lab jargon to anyone who is not her — which may be exactly the point, or exactly the problem.
- **Deep Meld** — the top ladder band as the title. **Zero records in any class**, second-cleanest of the eight, and it keeps "Meld" (load-bearing in the glossary) while dropping the exact blocked phrase. Two counts against: it would still face a similarity analysis against Reg. 7540401 in the same class, since it reads as a deliberate step away from the blocker; and it still names the win rather than the joke.

Historical note worth keeping: **Hasbro filed *BIO MELD* in Class 28 in 1996** and abandoned it. The MELD family has been circled by toy companies before.
